PRISM: PQC Accelerators for ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA and HQC

PRISM is an open-source chip that runs four post-quantum cryptography (PQC) standards in hardware: ML-KEM-1024 (FIPS 203), ML-DSA-44 (FIPS 204), SLH-DSA-128f (FIPS 205) and HQC-128. It is built in TSMC 65 nm LP and runs at 48 MHz. I led the design from RTL to final layout, and it taped out in September 2026.

A CV32E40X RISC-V core sends 85 custom instructions over the CV-X-IF coprocessor interface to eight coprocessor banks. Each algorithm is on the die twice: once unprotected and once protected with domain-oriented masking (DOM). To save area, the algorithms share one protected Keccak-f[1600] unit, and a Trivium generator supplies the masks. The chip also has 128 KB of SRAM. The unprotected custom instructions run 3 to 41 times faster than software on the same core.

PRISM was built to be measured. A scan chain covers all 30,301 flops, so the full internal state can be read out after a fault or during a leakage test. Because the protected and unprotected versions share one die, an evaluator can compare them with the same setup, without building a new target. The chips are expected in December 2026, so the silicon side-channel and fault evaluation is still ahead. The assessments published so far are pre-silicon and FPGA.

PRISM is the third generation of the coprocessor. The first carried 38 instructions for ML-KEM-1024 and ML-DSA-44 (OSCAR 2026). The second, the FPT 2026 accelerator, carried 62 and was assessed per instruction on a ChipWhisperer CW305. Background on the platform is on the openPQC accelerator page. The RTL is public as ospqc-pro.
