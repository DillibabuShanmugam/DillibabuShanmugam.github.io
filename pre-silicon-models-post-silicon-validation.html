Pre-Silicon Models to Post-Silicon Validation

Security bugs found after tape-out cost the most and are fixed the latest. The industry answer is shift-left: assess side-channel leakage and fault behavior on pre-silicon models, at RTL, gate level, or layout, before the chip exists. The open question is whether those models tell the truth. This research program treats post-silicon validation as a first-class problem: every pre-silicon prediction is checked against measurements on fabricated silicon, and the gap between model and measurement is itself the object of study.

On the side-channel side, pre-silicon leakage models (COSADE 2023) improve leakage assessment before fabrication, and transfer learning carries knowledge from simulated traces to measured ones, cutting the number of physical traces needed for an attack by 2.8 times and evaluation time by 30 percent. The approach was applied top-down to ML-DSA-87 on the openPQC platform, where pre-silicon models predicted the leakage later confirmed by correlation power analysis on the implementation.

On the fault side, FaultDetective (TCHES 2024) explains observed faults across the whole stack, from design layout through microarchitecture to software. muScan trains CNN and GNN models on scan-chain data to detect faulty microarchitectural states at 92 percent accuracy, turning the design-for-test infrastructure every chip already has into a security sensor. More than 330 electromagnetic fault-injection campaigns on RISC-V SoCs provide the post-silicon ground truth.

The silicon anchors are two TSMC test chips designed and taped out for exactly this purpose: CAPRI1, a RISC-V SoC for systematic electromagnetic fault characterization, and CAPRI6, a six-core lockstep SoC with a full scan chain for cycle-accurate fault root-cause analysis. Together they close the loop: models predict, silicon validates, and the validated models make the next design more secure before it is built.
