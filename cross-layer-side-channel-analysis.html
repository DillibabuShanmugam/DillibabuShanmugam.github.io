Cross-Layer Side-Channel Analysis, Pre- and Post-Silicon

A side-channel leak can be found at several layers of a design: in the RTL, in the gate netlist, in the layout, or in power measured on the finished device. Each layer is cheaper to test than the one after it, but only the last one is the truth. Cross-layer side-channel analysis runs the same test on pre-silicon models and on post-silicon measurements, then checks whether the verdicts agree.

The clearest result is the per-instruction assessment of a unified ML-KEM-1024 and ML-DSA-44 accelerator with 62 custom instructions (IEEE FPT 2026). I tested the ten instructions that touch secret data twice, with the same inputs: once from per-cycle RTL toggle counts and once from power traces on a ChipWhisperer CW305 FPGA board. A fixed-versus-random TVLA test at 500 runs per class gave the same verdict for all ten, and the two activity profiles correlated at a mean of 0.824. The bitstream is built with open-source tools only (Yosys, nextpnr-xilinx, Project X-Ray).

Earlier work set up the method. Pre-silicon leakage models (COSADE 2023) improve leakage assessment before fabrication, and transfer learning from simulated to measured traces cut the physical traces needed for an attack by 2.8 times and evaluation time by 30 percent. For ML-DSA-87, a top-down assessment on pre-silicon models located the leaking operations and recovered a subkey with correlation power analysis on simulated traces. I also contributed to SCAPEgoat, an open side-channel analysis library (IEEE VTS 2025).

This page covers the side-channel half of a wider program on pre-silicon models and post-silicon validation. The fault half is on the pre- and post-silicon fault analysis page.
